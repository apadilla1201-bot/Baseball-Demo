College recruiting

Getting recruited is a process. Most families start it two years late.

A roadmap by grad year, a profile built on numbers coaches trust, and an honest answer about the level you can actually pitch at.

The roadmap

By grad year.

  1. Freshman

    09

    Build the body. Build the habit.

    • Get in the weight room with a program, not a plan to "start lifting"
    • First baseline on radar and video. Nobody cares about the number yet; we do
    • Grades matter from day one. A 3.0 freshman year is a hole you dig out of for three years
    • Play for a travel team that gets you innings, not the one with the best jerseys

  2. Sophomore

    10

    Know your level. Start the list.

    • Honest conversation about level fit based on velo, body, academics and trajectory
    • Build the recruiting profile: verified metrics, 90-second video, transcript
    • First camps at schools on the list. Unofficial visits if you're within driving distance
    • Summer: one or two events where your target schools actually show up

  3. Junior

    11

    This is the year.

    • Email campaign to a list of 25–40 schools, updated after every retest
    • Showcases and camps picked by who'll be there, not by the brochure
    • Junior spring is the season coaches watch. Be healthy and be on the mound
    • Visits, conversations, and if the offer is right, the commitment

  4. Senior

    12

    Close it. Or pivot on purpose.

    • Uncommitted in September isn't a crisis. It's a different list: D2, D3, NAIA, JUCO with real opportunity
    • Keep training. Senior spring velo jumps happen and coaches still have spots in May
    • Academic signing, financial aid, the right questions about roster size and redshirts
    • If JUCO is the path, pick the one that moves pitchers to four-year schools

The profile

What coaches actually open.

Video
Ninety seconds. Open side, closed side, behind the catcher. Fastball, breaking ball, changeup. No music, no slow-motion montage. Radar in the frame if you have it. Updated every retest.
Verified metrics
Velo from an event or a facility a coach has heard of. Pitch metrics from a retest day. Height, weight, 60 time if you've got one. Dated.
Academics
GPA, test scores if you've taken them, intended major. For D3 and high-academic D1, this is half the profile.
The one-pager
Name, grad year, hand, high school, travel team, contact for you and your parents, coach references, link to video. One page. PDF.

Showcases & camps

Spend the money where the coaches are.

South Florida has more showcases than anywhere in the country, and most families waste three to five thousand dollars on the wrong ones. The rule: go where the schools on your list will be, when you're ready to post a number you're proud of. Camps at target schools are worth more than most showcases. A bad showcase number lives on the internet for a year.

  1. 01Sophomore summer: one regional event for a baseline and a verified number
  2. 02Junior fall: camps at two or three target schools where you've already emailed the pitching coach
  3. 03Junior summer: the one or two national events your target level actually scouts at
  4. 04Senior fall: uncommitted showcases if needed, picked by who's attending

Emailing coaches

Short. Specific. Followed up.

Coaches get hundreds of these. Yours gets opened if the subject line has your grad year, position and a number, and the body fits on a phone screen.

Follow-up rule: every retest, every new number, every camp registration is a reason to email again. Three touches before you move a school down the list.

Level fit

Where does 85 play? Honestly.

Velocity ranges are typical for pitchers who get recruited, not minimums, and they move every year. Command, secondary pitches, body and academics shift a pitcher up or down a level.

LevelTypical RHP veloWhat matters beyond veloWho it fits
D188–94+Present stuff, projectable body, track record against good hittersThe top of the pyramid. Fewer spots than the Miami travel scene suggests
D284–90Command, a real secondary pitch, strong Florida D2 conference exposureA lot of South Florida pitchers. Scholarship money is real here
D380–87Academics first. Command, competitiveness, coachabilityHigh-academic pitchers who want the degree and still want to compete
NAIA82–89Similar to D2. Smaller schools, flexible on eligibility, often more moneyPitchers who want to play right away and keep developing
JUCO80–90Innings. Development. A proven path to a four-year rosterLate bloomers, academic repairs, and anyone who needs two years of starts

Eligibility and recruiting-contact rules are set by each association and change often. We'll point you to the current NCAA, NAIA and NJCAA guides rather than quote rules here.

Parent guide

What's yours to do, and what isn't.

Yours

  • Transcript, test registration, financial-aid paperwork
  • Travel and camp budget decisions
  • Being in the room on visits, asking about roster size, redshirts and medical staff
  • Keeping the timeline honest when the athlete gets discouraged

Not yours

  • Emailing coaches. It has to come from the athlete
  • Negotiating with the coach in front of your son
  • Chasing the D1 logo over the right fit
  • Posting velo numbers on social media that we haven't verified
