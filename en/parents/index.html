Safety & arm health

The rules I don't bend.

  1. 01

    No throwing through pain.

    Soreness that lasts beyond 48 hours stops the throwing plan until we know why. If it's not obvious, you see a doctor before we resume. I'll help you find one who works with throwers.

  2. 02

    Workload is tracked.

    Every athlete logs throws per week, including school and travel ball. If the total is unsafe for his age, I'll tell you, and I'll tell you which team needs to hear it.

  3. 03

    Weighted balls are earned.

    No weighted-ball pulldowns before a strength baseline and a clean mobility screen. Never in-season. Never for pitchers under fifteen.

  4. 04

    Return-to-throw is written with the medical team.

    Pitchers coming off injury start with a plan their doctor and PT have seen. Six phases. No mound until phase four.

  5. 05

    You can always watch.

    Parents are welcome in the facility at every session. There's a bench and Wi-Fi.

Recruiting timeline

When things happen, so you can plan the budget.

  1. 01

    Freshman year

    Weight room, baseline, grades. Travel team that gets innings. Budget: training only.

  2. 02

    Sophomore summer

    Level-fit conversation. First regional event for a verified number. Budget: one event, camp at a local school.

  3. 03

    Junior fall

    Profile goes live. Email campaign starts. Camps at two or three target schools.

  4. 04

    Junior spring

    The season coaches watch. Health first, innings second, velo third.

  5. 05

    Junior summer

    One or two national events matched to your level. Visits. Offers for D1 and top D2 often land here.

  6. 06

    Senior fall

    Commit, or pivot on purpose to D2, D3, NAIA, JUCO. Most of my commits happen between September and February of senior year.
