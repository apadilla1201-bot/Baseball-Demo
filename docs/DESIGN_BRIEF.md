# Design brief — Rivas Pitching Co.

## Brand name (3 options, one chosen)

1. **Rivas Pitching Co.** — A surname brand. Single-coach businesses live or die on the
   coach's name and reputation, so the name *is* the proof. Works in English and Spanish
   without translation, reads like a real shop, and nothing in the Miami market uses it.
   **Chosen.**
2. **Biscayne Arm Works** — Place-based and sounds established, but "Biscayne" skews
   toward the tourist map. Families in Hialeah, Kendall and Doral don't identify with it.
3. **Eighth Street Pitching** — A Calle Ocho nod with a strong local story, but it names a
   neighborhood the facility isn't in, and the brand would be fighting its own geography.

Checked by web search: no existing "Rivas Pitching" business in Miami-Dade; no known
pitching coach named Danny Rivas in Miami or Doral.

## The coach (invented)

Danny Rivas, RHP. Miami Southridge HS → FIU → five seasons of affiliated minor-league ball,
topped out at Double-A. Coaching since 2015. 38 college commits since 2017. Average
in-program fastball gain: +4.1 mph over six months (reported as a program average, not a
promise). 300+ pitchers trained. Bilingual household, runs every lesson in English or
Spanish, whichever the family speaks.

Facility: a warehouse bay in Doral (placeholder: 7800 NW 25th St, Unit 4, Doral, FL 33122).
Portable mound, radar, high-speed video, plyo wall. Remote program for athletes outside
South Florida.

## Art direction

**Feel.** Editorial sports brand. Think a well-funded independent training shop, not a
startup. Confident, a little blunt, specific. Nothing glossy.

**Type.**
- Display: *Barlow Condensed* 600–800, tight tracking, uppercase for the biggest lines.
  Condensed type reads like scoreboard and jersey lettering without imitating either.
- Body: *Archivo* (variable). Neutral grotesque, comfortable at 16–18px.
- Data: *IBM Plex Mono* for every number that matters — velo readings, dates, grad years,
  timestamps. Numbers set in mono tell the reader they're measurements, not marketing.

**Color.** One restrained palette, one accent.
- Ink `#121212` (near black, warm), Paper `#F1EDE4` (bone, warm), Chalk `#FFFFFF`
- Accent: Clay `#D9481C` — mound dirt and infield clay. Used for one thing per screen:
  the live number, the active state, the line you should read first.
- Grays: `#2A2A2A`, `#6B6761`, `#B8B2A7`. No gradients. No glass.

**Layout.** Asymmetric 12-column grid. Sections alternate paper / ink, and each one has its
own structure: a two-column selector, a stat strip with a rule, a full-bleed photo with a
caption block hanging off the grid, a table, a timeline, a commit board that looks like a
board. Sharp corners everywhere; the only radius is on the pill-shaped language toggle.
Thin 1px rules do the work shadows would otherwise do.

**Imagery.** One photo set, one grade: Miami overcast-bright daylight, warm shadows,
slightly desaturated, light film grain. Pitchers in plain gear, no logos. College athletes
in solid-color uniforms with no lettering. The coach appears in black tee and plain cap in
every shot. Reject anything with hand or seam artifacts.

**Motion.** Stat count-ups on first view, a velo line that draws itself on the Results page,
the step indicator on the intake. Nothing else moves.

**Miami, without the postcard.** Bilingual toggle in the nav, not buried in a footer. Copy
references travel ball, Perfect Game, FHSAA seasons, summer heat, year-round throwing.
Addresses, phone formats and schedules look like Miami-Dade. No palms, no sunsets, no
flamingos, no neon.

## How it avoids looking AI-generated

- No centered hero with two side-by-side buttons. Hero is a left-weighted headline over a
  full-bleed photo with a data strip along the bottom edge.
- No three-identical-cards-with-icons grids. Lists are lists; comparisons are tables.
- No icons as decoration, no emoji, no gradients, no glow, no uniform rounded corners.
- Copy sounds like a coach who has had this conversation 300 times: short, specific,
  sometimes blunt. Banned phrases are banned.
- Real-looking details everywhere: radar readings, pitch metrics with units, grad years,
  school names, a weekly schedule, a commit board with dates.
